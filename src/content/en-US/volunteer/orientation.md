# Volunteer Orientation

Bicycle Kitchen is a volunteer-run community bike space! We hope to promote the bicycle as a fun, safe, and accessible form of transportation, to foster healthy urban communities, and to provide a welcoming space to learn about building, maintaining, and riding bicycles. 

## History

Bicycle Kitchen started in 2002 in an unused kitchen at the LA Eco-Village. We moved to Melrose and Heliotrope (Mel-Hel/Bicycle District) in 2005 before finally buying the current shop on Fountain in 2012.

## Organization

We operate on consensus. For us, consensus means making decisions by synthesizing people’s ideas and opinions. It’s a messy and continuous process. Reach out for zine recs on consensus!

We have a meeting every month, where we try to make big decisions together.

There’s no paid staff at the Kitchen. We have two volunteer roles - shadows and cooks. The main difference is that cooks have a key to open and close the shop. Some shadows become cooks. Some shadows volunteer for years and prefer not having a key.

## Programming

### Shop Hours
We focus on helping people learn to fix their bikes. We provide the tools, used parts, and volunteer knowledge to help with *most* repairs.

### Project Bikes
We accept bike donations each week. When someone comes in looking for a bike, we connect them to their own project bike that they work on and leave at the Kitchen until it’s ready to be taken home.

### Mobile Repair
We set-up repair stands at events around LA, collaborating with groups like the Hollywood Food Coalition, CicLAvia, and LA Food Not Bombs.

### Fundraisers + Shows
We host fundraisers with local groups, like LA Food Not Bombs and Fairfax Mutual Aid. Usually these take the form of a party or concert. We also host a movie screening every month.

### Groups Rides + Fun
We sometimes ride up to the helipad at Griffith Park or out to get sandwiches. We host feeder rides to fun events, like the All-City Toy Ride, and a party before the LA Marathon Crash Ride.

### Workshops
We host fix-a-flat and basic repair workshops. Wild Wolf Cycling Club hosts Wild Wrench at the Kitchen, a monthly workshop.

Aside from our shop hours, which we try to be dependable with, lots of our programming is ad hoc and comes together when volunteers decide to make it happen! This is especially true of our workshops, orientations, fundraisers, and group rides.

## How a Shift Works

### Overview

Our shop is open for 10 or so hours each week. Typically, people come by to repair their own bike, find or work on a project bike, or donate bikes.

We set up each client with a repair stand. We keep tools behind the counter and rely on volunteers to fetch tools for clients. We have used parts scattered around the shop, mostly in the big metal drawers. If you can’t find something, ask another volunteer!

Our volunteers float around, helping clients with their repairs. We do not do repairs for clients!

### Welcoming A Client

We like to check-in with people when they arrive to ask what they want to work on. You can give clients a rundown on what to expect - where to find used parts, who to ask for help or tools, how our sliding scale donations work, and that they’ll be doing their own repairs, with guidance.

### Setting Expectations

It’s important to set realistic expectations about what a client is likely to achieve, provided the repair, our volunteer capacity, and the time before closing. If someone has a bunch of projects, ask them to set an order of priority!

If the shop is busy, you’ll want to note that it may be slow to get guidance and/or tools.

It’s also good to check in about budget before starting repairs. Everything is sliding scale, and we work with whatever someone can afford. Bringing it up early can help avoid confusion later when settling up.

### Things To Do

Here’s a list of things that people will do during a shift:

### Opening the shop

- Grab bike stands from the closet and set them up outside  
- Open the register  
- Sweep and clean up workspaces  
- Put away unsorted tools

### During shift

- Welcome clients, ask about their needs  
- Answer the phone  
- Help clients with repairs  
- Repair a donated bike  
- Ring up clients  
- Sort donations  
- Eat food  
- Go for a walk  
- Talk to other volunteers  
- Ask questions about repairs

### Closing the shop

- Put bike stands back in the closet  
- Turn off the air compressor  
- Close the register, print balances  
- Turn off the lights  
- Put tools and parts away

## Donations

No one is turned away from the Bike Kitchen for lack of funds! We can work with anyone’s budget.

### How much does X cost?
We suggest $8/hour of stand time, i.e. time spent in the shop. For new and used parts, you can search in Lightspeed for an item’s suggested price. You may want to adjust the suggested price, like if a part is super high end. Ultimately, though, all of our suggested prices, including for stand time, fall on a sliding scale that depends on a client’s budget.

When pricing a project bike, it’s best to first ask the client what their budget is. Then, typically 2-3 cooks will come up with a suggested donation and agree on a single amount together.

### What forms of payment do we accept?
Credit card, debit card, cash, Venmo (@BicycleKitchen), PayPal, and work trade.

### What’s work trade?
Work trade is when someone volunteers in exchange for credits. Those credits are typically applied toward a project bike. Each work trade hour is credited at $10. Work trades should only be used when the donation wanted for a bike is more than a client’s budget. The number of work trade hours suggested for a project bike should reflect that $10/hour isn’t a reasonable amount to pay someone for their labor.

### Do we accept trades of used parts, tools, bikes, etc.?
No, clients can’t trade in bikes/parts in exchange for other bikes/parts. I’m not hip to why this is the case. My guess is that because the BK has monetary expenses, like mortgage, utilities, and insurance, accepting goods as payment would make it hard to meet these expenses.

### Can someone buy just parts?
No. Since we’re focused on bike repair education, we don’t sell used parts on their own. A client would need to bring their bike in and complete the repair in the shop in order to make use of the used parts.

### Do we accept in-kind donations?
Yes, we’ll take bikes, used parts, tools, etc. If a bike is rusted beyond repair, we’d prefer to not take it. Just use your judgment! We end up taking most donations.

### Do we give tax-receipts for in-kind donations?
Yes. A PDF of the tax receipt document can be found on the desktop of the computer. Once you print it out, you only need to fill out your name, the date, and the list of items donated. The donor can fill out any other information in their own time, including the value of the donation.

## Shadowing FAQs

### Who is a shadow?
Anyone coming by to help out, that isn’t a cook, is a shadow.

### What does a shadow do?  
Shadows don't literally have to *shadow*, although that can be a great way to learn and build trust when you’re new to the space. You can work on donated bikes, help clients with repairs you’re familiar with, or come up with new workshops. It’s open-ended!

### Can shadows help clients with their bikes?
Yep! At first, try working alongside cooks as they help out clients. When you’re comfortable, check in with the shifting cooks about working 1-1 with clients.

### What if I’m unsure of how to fix a bike problem?
Ask! It’s way way better to check with another volunteer if you’re unsure of something than to pretend. We’re all learning from one another.

### Someone is doing XYZ wrong, should I tell them?
Please avoid giving people unsolicited advice. It’s an all too common experience to work on your bike at a coop and have 5 different people come over and tell you what to do. If someone is doing something you consider unsafe or would damage our tools, though, please interject.

This said, you should feel free to check in with people and ask what they nede, as far as guidance and tools!

### I shouldn’t touch other people or their bikes without their permission, right? 
Correct.

### Can I work on my own bike at the Bicycle Kitchen?**  
Yes, definitely. It’s best to either work on your own bike or volunteer - but not do both at the same time. Just let one of the other volunteers know what your plan is when you arrive.

### I’m not getting paid, right?**  
Yes, no one is being paid to be at the Bike Kitchen! Take breaks whenever you like. Leave early, come late. Chat with the other volunteers. Make sure you’re enjoying yourself.

### Do I need to sign-up for a shift?
Nope, just drop-by.

### When should I volunteer?
Coming routinely on a particular day of the week is a great way to help keep the space open and connect with other volunteers! Perhaps try out a few shifts and see which group of volunteers you vibe with.

### Who do I contact about X?
We have a Google Group for shadows, called our shadows list. All of the cooks are on the shadows list, too. If you have a BK-wide question or announcement, feel free to send it to the shadows list. Otherwise, exchange contact info with the other volunteers or maybe start a Signal group for new volunteers!

## Common BK Woes

### I feel like I’m just standing around
That’s okay! If you like standing around, you should feel free to keep doing so! Lots of learning and fun can come from observing on-going repairs and popping in to ask questions.

If you don’t like standing around, you’re always welcome to ask a cook or other shadow if they know of any projects you could help with. Tasks almost always in need of doing include sorting tools, tidying up the back wall, and putting away errant used parts. You can also grab a bike off the wall and wrench on that…

### I want to fix bikes, not sort inner tubes! 
You’re welcome to work on the bikes that have been donated to the BK! Just as long a bike hasn’t been claimed for someone else’s project.

Fixing up donated bikes makes it easier for future clients to walk out with functional bikes. And it’s one of the best ways to become more comfortable with repairs

Fixing up donated bikes can be tricky if the shop is very busy. If the shop is quiet enough and you can find a clear space, ideally outside, go for it!


## More Than Wrenching

There is so much more to the Bike Kitchen than helping folks with bike repairs. There’s an ever disorganized and sparse assemblage of tools. Good vibes that need maintaining. Parties and shows. Group rides. If you have an idea for contributing to the space, talk with other volunteers and go for it! The Bike Kitchen is co-created by all of its volunteers, and its practices are not as organized as you might expect (or hope).
